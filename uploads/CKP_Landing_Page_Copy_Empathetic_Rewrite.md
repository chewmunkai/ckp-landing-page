## Section 1

*“My accountant and company secretary handle all this.”*

## They probably do. The real question is whether anyone sees the whole company.

You hired professionals because you cannot personally track every tax instalment, SSM filing, payroll deadline and employee contribution while also running the business. That is not a mistake. It is what a business owner is supposed to do.

But each person usually sees only one part of the picture. Your accountant works from the records provided. Your company secretary handles statutory filings. Payroll handles employee contributions.

Each person may be doing their job correctly—and you can still be left wondering whether everything connects.

When something is missed, the letter does not go to the gap between those providers. It comes to the company and, in many cases, to the director named on the SSM register.

That does not mean you need to do everyone else’s job. It means you should be able to answer three simple questions:

**Are we okay? What is due next? Is anything missing?**

This webinar is designed to help you answer them.

---

## Section 2

## The biggest risk is not always bad advice. It is good people working from different information.

This is how many Malaysian SMEs operate. The books may sit in Excel or with a part-time bookkeeper. A company secretary handles SSM. Someone else manages EPF, SOCSO and payroll. Compliance gets reviewed properly only when the audit starts.

There is nothing unusual about that setup. The problem is what happens between the handovers.

The bookkeeper may not know three employees were hired last month. The payroll person may not know the financial year end is wrong. The company secretary may not know the books cannot support the company’s tax estimate.

Nobody is necessarily careless. Nobody is necessarily doing poor work. But unless someone connects the information, small gaps can sit quietly for months.

**And small gaps become expensive when they finally surface.**

| When this is missed | What can follow |
|---|---|
| CP204 instalment, due by the 15th of each month | 10% of the unpaid instalment added without notice |
| EPF, SOCSO and EIS, due by the 15th of each month | Late charges accruing daily from the due date |
| Employer registration before the first hire | Contributions backdated to day one across all three funds |
| Keeping the books clean and current | Missed deductions, rejected loans and tenders you cannot apply for |

You should not have to chase three different people just to understand one company.

What closes the gaps is a connected view of the company secretary work, the books and payroll—so each part works from the same information. That is what CKP will walk you through during this session.
